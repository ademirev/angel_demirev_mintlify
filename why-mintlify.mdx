## The Mission Resonates Personally

Mintlify's mission is to empower builders — to make the documentation layer of software so good that it accelerates how fast people can build, learn, and ship. I've spent my entire career in the infrastructure layer of people operations for exactly that kind of company. At OpenAI and Tools for Humanity, my job was to build the systems that let engineers, researchers, and operators move fast without losing integrity. The parallelism is real: I build people infrastructure the same way Mintlify builds docs infrastructure — with care for the end user, obsession over reliability, and a bias toward automation over manual process.

## The Inflection Point Is Familiar

Mintlify just passed 65 employees and closed a $45M Series B. I know this moment well. I joined OpenAI at a similar inflection — a company that had proven its core product, raised serious capital, and was about to scale faster than its people infrastructure could support. The gap between "we've been winging it and it's mostly worked" and "we need real systems or this breaks" is exactly where I operate best. The cost of getting it wrong compounds fast: comp bands that don't hold up under scrutiny, performance processes that feel arbitrary, compliance gaps that become liabilities. I've seen what happens when you get ahead of that, and I've seen what happens when you don't.

## On the Stack

I want to be specific about the tooling because it matters. Rippling is a platform that rewards operators who understand its data model — the companies that get the most out of it are the ones who invest early in role-based access controls, custom workflows, and clean integration architecture. Ashby is built for recruiting teams that want to be data-driven, and the value compounds when you connect it cleanly to your HRIS. Deel abstracts a lot of global employment complexity, but you still need someone who understands the underlying compliance landscape to use it responsibly. I've worked in adjacent stacks at OpenAI and Tools for Humanity and understand these tools at a level that goes beyond checkbox familiarity.

## What I Want to Build

If I'm honest about what excites me most, it's this: the opportunity to build a people function that feels like a Mintlify product — clean, well-documented, reliable, and genuinely useful to the people who depend on it. That means compensation bands people trust, performance processes that feel fair and legible, onboarding that actually prepares someone to do their best work, and data infrastructure that gives leadership an honest picture of org health. It means building the kind of people infrastructure that doesn't make headlines because it never breaks.

That's the work. I'm ready to do it.
