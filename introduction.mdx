I've spent my career at the intersection of people strategy and systems thinking — not because I stumbled into it, but because I believe the best HR work is invisible when it's done right and catastrophic when it isn't. At Google, OpenAI, and Tools for Humanity, I've had the privilege of building the infrastructure that lets people do their best work. Now I'm looking for a place where I can do that from the ground up, with founders who care about getting it right from the start. Mintlify is that place.

My path started at Google, where I spent six years learning the craft — first as a People Ops Coordinator, then as a Compensation Design Analyst, and eventually as a Compensation Partner advising some of Alphabet's most ambitious bets. Those years gave me the analytical foundation and the philosophical grounding that I carry everywhere: compensation is a signal, not just a number. How you pay people tells them what you value.

OpenAI was where I got to build. I joined in August 2022, when the company was still finding its footing operationally, and left in early 2025 having built a team that owned systems, benefits, analytics, and global expansion. I led our Workday implementation from business case through go-live. I launched a data warehouse and maintained the ETL pipelines, dashboards, and role-based access controls that turned raw HRIS data into decisions. I automated manual data movement via APIs because I genuinely enjoy that kind of problem-solving — and because it frees up my team to do higher-leverage work. When I was promoted to Director of HR Operations, Systems, and Analytics in late 2023, I wasn't just managing a function; I was building one in real time, inside one of the most consequential companies in the world.

At Tools for Humanity, I've continued that pattern. I led another Workday implementation — this time across IT, Legal, Finance, and People simultaneously. I built a data warehouse that enables natural language analyses and data integrity monitoring. I rebuilt the performance and compensation cycles with evidence-based feedback structures that meaningfully reduced pay variance. And I've used Claude Code and Codex to build automations for award communication and letter distribution, because when you're operating at scale, the details matter and the tooling should handle them.

I want to be direct about something: I'm not looking for a Head of People role because it's the next title on the ladder. I'm looking for a specific kind of opportunity — one where I can build the People function from a clean foundation, partner closely with founders who are thoughtful about culture, and bring the same builder instinct I've applied to every systems challenge to the human side of the organization. The work I'm most proud of has always been the work that nobody sees — the onboarding flow that just works, the comp cycle that feels fair, the data pipeline that catches an anomaly before it becomes a problem.

That's what I want to build at Mintlify. A people function that's as well-engineered as the product itself.
