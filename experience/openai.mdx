I joined OpenAI in August 2022, three months before ChatGPT launched and permanently changed both the company's trajectory and the world's relationship with AI. What followed was one of the most intense scaling environments in recent tech history: headcount growing rapidly, international expansion accelerating, complexity compounding faster than most organizations' people infrastructure could keep pace with. My time at OpenAI spanned two roles over two and a half years, and the through-line across both was the same challenge — build systems, automations, and team cultures that could scale alongside the company without becoming the bottleneck.

## Roles at OpenAI

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Director, HR Operations">
    ### Scope

    Promoted to Director of HR Operations, Systems, and Analytics in November 2023, taking on leadership of a team spanning people systems, benefits, HR operations, and global expansion. The scope was broad by design: at OpenAI's growth rate, these functions needed to move in close coordination, and the team needed a leader who could drive both strategy and execution across all of them.

    The organizational challenge wasn't just volume — it was complexity. New countries, new legal entities, new benefit schemes, an evolving workforce composition, and a leadership team that needed reliable people data to make fast decisions. Lean resourcing throughout.

    ### Key Accomplishments

    **People Analytics Foundation**
    Built OpenAI's people data warehouse from the ground up, establishing ETL pipelines that pulled from Workday, the ATS, equity systems, and other HR tools into a unified, queryable data layer. Implemented role-based access controls so that the right stakeholders — from recruiters to executives — could access relevant data without requiring a data team intermediary for every request. Launched dashboards that gave leadership self-service visibility into headcount, attrition, offer acceptance, and comp distribution.

    **Data Integrity at Scale**
    Deployed automated monitoring for data integrity anomalies across the People data ecosystem — catching mismatches, missing records, and data drift before they surfaced as errors in payroll, reporting, or compliance. This wasn't just a technical project; it changed the team's operating model from reactive firefighting to proactive auditing. The result was a measurable improvement in data quality and a significant reduction in the time spent investigating and correcting errors.

    **Global Expansion Infrastructure**
    Managed the people infrastructure build-out for OpenAI's international expansion, including benefits design, employment frameworks, and operational processes for new countries. Worked cross-functionally with Legal, Finance, and external counsel to ensure compliant, scalable setups.

    ### Team Culture

    The culture I tried to build on this team had a few defining characteristics: **autonomy** (people owned their domains and made decisions without waiting for permission), **psychological safety** (bad news traveled fast, experimentation was encouraged, mistakes were learned from), **systems thinking** (every problem was worth understanding at the root before solving at the surface), and **continuous technical development** (I actively pushed team members to expand their tooling and technical skills, because lean teams can only scale if individuals are multiplying their own leverage). Several team members grew significantly in scope and technical capability during this period — which I consider one of the most meaningful outcomes of the role.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Comp, Benefits & Ops Manager">
    ### Scope

    Joined OpenAI as Compensation, Benefits, and Operations Manager in August 2022 — before the ChatGPT moment, when the company was already ambitious but the scale of what was coming wasn't yet visible. The role covered the full operational and rewards stack: comp philosophy, benefits design and vendor management, and the operational infrastructure underneath both.

    ### Key Accomplishments

    **Workday Implementation**
    Led OpenAI's Workday implementation from initial business case through go-live — including requirements gathering, vendor coordination, configuration, data migration, testing, and change management. This was a significant cross-functional effort, touching IT, Finance, Legal, and every corner of the People team. The outcome was a modern HRIS foundation that could support OpenAI's growing workforce and international complexity, replacing a system that had hit its limits.

    **Incentive Design from First Principles**
    Partnered with executives to reimagine OpenAI's compensation philosophy from the ground up rather than iterating on what already existed. That meant addressing benchmarking methodology, equity design and refresh practices, geographic pay localization, and job architecture — all of which needed to be coherent, defensible, and competitive in one of the most aggressive talent markets in history. Built and maintained the comp infrastructure (salary bands, equity ranges, leveling frameworks) that governed thousands of offers and compensation decisions.

    **API-Based Automation**
    Built tooling to move data across people systems via their APIs, replacing manual exports, copy-paste, and email chains with automated pipelines. The processes this unlocked included offer approvals and offer extensions, compensation letter issuance, equity processing workflows, benefits data reconciliation, and new-hire onboarding. The cumulative efficiency gain was substantial — and more importantly, it reduced the error surface that came with manual data handling.

    **Benefits Design and Vendor Management**
    Refined plan design across health, dental, vision, and ancillary benefits. Managed vendors, led RFPs to improve service quality and cost efficiency, and launched benefits programs in new countries as OpenAI expanded internationally. The goal was a benefits package that was genuinely competitive — not just checkbox-complete — and vendor relationships where OpenAI was treated as a priority client.

    **Recruiter Enablement on Compensation**
    Hosted monthly trainings and 1-on-1 coaching sessions with the recruiting team on compensation fundamentals — how bands are built, how to read a comp offer relative to the market, how to navigate pushback from candidates, and how to use comp as a competitive lever rather than a script. The goal was a recruiting team that could hold a sophisticated comp conversation with any candidate, which improved close rates on competitive offers.
  </Tab>

<Tip>
  The most important thing I learned scaling People at OpenAI is that **the limiting factor is almost never budget — it's systems and judgment**. With lean teams and exponential growth, you cannot hire your way out of operational debt. You have to build infrastructure that multiplies the leverage of each person on the team. That means investing early in data systems, automating the repeatable, and developing team members who think in systems rather than tasks. Every new tool or process should answer the question: does this let us do more, or does it just give us more to manage?
</Tip>
