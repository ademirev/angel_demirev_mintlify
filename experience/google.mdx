Six years at Google were formative in ways I still draw on daily. I joined in 2016 as a People Ops Coordinator and Compensation Design Analyst — learning the mechanics of compensation from one of the most sophisticated total rewards organizations in the world — and grew into a Compensation Partner role advising Alphabet's autonomous pre-IPO companies. These weren't divisions of Google; they were separate operating companies (Waymo, Verily, and peers) building independent compensation programs while navigating the complexity of sitting under a public parent company. The work was substantive, the clients were demanding, and the breadth of companies I supported in parallel gave me a kind of pattern recognition across compensation problems that's hard to develop any other way.

## Compensation Partner: Advising Alphabet's Pre-IPO Companies

As a Compensation Partner from November 2019 through July 2022, I served as a dedicated advisor to the HR and Finance teams at multiple Alphabet autonomous companies — each with its own headcount, competitive landscape, talent strategy, and relationship to Alphabet's corporate comp infrastructure.

### The Breadth of Work

Running compensation programs across six companies simultaneously meant managing different cycles, different band structures, different equity practices, and different executive relationships — all at once. The work covered every layer of the compensation stack:

- **Philosophy and design:** Partnered with business leaders and HR at each company to develop compensation philosophies that reflected their talent strategies, competitive positioning, and stage of growth. Pre-IPO companies have specific constraints — equity liquidity, public comparables, retaining employees through the pre/post-IPO transition — and the comp design had to account for all of it.
- **Benchmarking:** Used Croner, Mercer, and Radford GTS — as well as pre-IPO-specific survey sources — to benchmark custom jobs that didn't map cleanly to standard survey cuts. Partnered with business leaders to validate market matches and document the rationale behind internal placements.
- **Cyclical programs:** Orchestrated the full annual comp planning process across six companies — communications, manager training, budget modeling and approval, implementation of custom business rules in Google Sheets, oversight through the discretionary planning period, and pay equity analyses post-cycle. Six companies in parallel means six sets of deadlines, stakeholders, and edge cases running simultaneously.
- **Comp committee materials:** Authored materials for quarterly compensation committee meetings, surfacing people health metrics — attrition risk, comp distribution, offer competitiveness, equity refresh utilization — for board-level audiences.

### Technical Skills: SQL and Systematic Thinking

One differentiator I developed at Google was a reputation for technical fluency in an environment where most comp and HR partners were working exclusively in spreadsheets. I learned SQL and used it to pull and analyze comp data directly — faster, more precisely, and with better audit trails than the export-and-manipulate workflow most partners relied on.

More importantly, I coached other partners across Google and the Alphabet Bets on SQL skills, running informal sessions to reduce their reliance on Google Sheets for analysis. This wasn't just skills transfer — it was a bet that technically stronger partners would make better decisions and be less dependent on data teams for routine analysis. That bet proved right, and it shaped how I think about technical fluency as a People team capability everywhere I've worked since.

## Foundation Years: Compensation Design Analyst and People Ops Coordinator

Before the Compensation Partner role, I spent three-plus years in the Compensation Design Analyst and People Ops Coordinator function — learning the infrastructure that makes compensation programs work at scale. This meant understanding survey methodology from the inside, learning how job architectures are built and maintained, supporting the operational processes that run under every comp cycle, and developing the attention to detail that high-stakes compensation work requires. These years were the foundation. Everything since has been built on them.
