---
title: "My 90-Day Plan: What I Would Build First at Mintlify"
sidebarTitle: "90-Day Plan"
description: "A concrete, phase-by-phase plan for the first 90 days as Mintlify's Head of People — from listening and learning to executing on quick wins."
---

The first 90 days at a 65-person company at this growth stage are not the time to arrive with a pre-packaged People strategy. They're the time to listen hard, map what's real, build the infrastructure that makes good decisions possible, and execute on the highest-leverage quick wins before momentum slips. This plan is how I'd do that at Mintlify — specifically, not generically.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Days 1–30">
    The first 30 days are about earning the right to have opinions. I don't know Mintlify's culture from the inside yet — I know what's visible from the outside, which is genuinely impressive, but it's not the same as knowing where the real friction lives. The goal of this phase is to create a clear, honest picture of the people landscape before building anything on top of it.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="1:1s with every employee — all 65">
        Structured listening sessions with every person in the company, not just leadership. I use a consistent question set (what's working, what's not, what would you change if you could, what do you worry about) so the data is comparable, but leave room for the conversation to go where it needs to go. This is the highest-ROI thing I can do in the first 30 days. By the time I'm done, I'll have a map of the culture that no survey can replicate — who the informal leaders are, where the trust gaps are, which teams have manager problems, and what employees need that they're not getting.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Audit the people stack">
        A thorough review of how Rippling, Ashby, and Deel are actually configured versus how they could be. This means looking at data flows, integration health, what's being tracked and what's falling through the cracks, and where manual work is filling gaps that automation should be filling. I'll come in with a fresh set of eyes on a stack I know well, and the audit output will feed directly into the automation priorities in phase three.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Review the employee handbook and compliance posture by jurisdiction">
        Line-by-line review of the current handbook against the jurisdictions where Mintlify has employees or contractors — including anything flowing through Deel. I'm looking for gaps, outdated language, and any areas where the company is exposed without knowing it. I'll flag critical issues immediately and build the full remediation plan in phase two.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Shadow the exec team">
        I want to understand how decisions actually get made before I try to influence them. This means attending leadership meetings, understanding the operating cadence, watching how the founders engage with the team, and developing a real read on what the culture is versus what it says it is. Mintlify's "slope over y-intercept" value is either a real operating principle or a recruiting line — I'll know within two weeks.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Audit compensation data">
        Pull the full comp picture: salary, equity, tenure, level, and role. I'm looking for internal variance that can't be explained by performance or scope, benchmark gaps on roles that are at retention risk, and equity refresh gaps for employees who are hitting cliff or vest completion points. I won't make any recommendations yet — this phase is diagnostic. But by day 30 I'll know where the compensation risk is.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Deliver: Initial people-health snapshot to founders by end of day 30">
        A concise, honest summary of what I found — what's working, where the risk is, and what I plan to do about it. This is not a polished deck designed to make everything sound fine. It's a candid read of the people landscape based on 65 conversations and a full audit of the infrastructure. The founders will learn something from it. That's the point.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Tip>
      If I could only do one thing in the first 30 days, it would be the 1:1s. Every other output in this phase gets better when it's grounded in what I actually heard from employees. The handbook audit is more useful when I know which policies employees find confusing. The comp audit is more urgent when I know which employees are fielding outside offers. The people-health snapshot is more credible when the founders know it reflects real conversations. Start with listening.
    </Tip>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Days 31–60">
    Phase two is about building the foundation — the systems, infrastructure, and frameworks that make the rest of the work possible. The listening is done. Now I'm translating it into structure. This phase is less visible externally but is the highest-leverage work I'll do at Mintlify.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Design and launch structured listening infrastructure">
        A listening program that actually works isn't a once-a-year engagement survey. It's a layered system: a short pulse survey every 4–6 weeks (5 questions, not 50), an exit interview framework that produces comparable data over time, and a manager effectiveness survey that gives managers real feedback on a regular cadence. I'll design all three in this phase, calibrate the questions against what I heard in the 1:1s, and launch the first pulse survey before day 60. The goal is to have a repeatable, low-burden listening system operational before I'm three months in.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Begin compensation audit: external benchmark comparison">
        Using the internal data from phase one and external benchmarks (I know Radford/Mercer cold from my Google years, and I'll determine which survey source fits Mintlify's stage and talent market), I'll run a full comparison — where are we paying competitively, where are we above market and creating unsustainable precedent, and where are we below market and losing talent we don't know we're losing yet. I'll also map equity refresh gaps and flag any employees in the critical vesting windows. The recommendations come in phase three; the analysis is complete by day 60.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Stand up weekly people-health read for the exec team">
        A standing artifact — concise, signal-rich, and actionable — delivered every week to the founders and exec team. Not a report they skim; a read they actually use. I'll design the format collaboratively with the founders based on what they want to see, but I'll push back if the instinct is to make it longer or more comprehensive. The value is in the consistency and the curation, not the volume.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Identify the top 3 compliance gaps and create a remediation plan">
        Coming out of the handbook review and jurisdiction audit, I'll prioritize the three areas of highest legal exposure and build a clear remediation plan for each: owner, timeline, and what "done" looks like. Some of these will be quick fixes (policy language, missing acknowledgments); others will require legal counsel and a longer timeline. I'll be clear about the difference.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Map the manager layer">
        Based on the 1:1s, I'll have a clear picture of which managers are thriving, which are struggling in ways they're aware of, and which are struggling in ways they don't know yet. I'll map the manager layer formally — tenure as a manager, team size, skip-level sentiment, and self-reported confidence — and use that map to design the coaching program in phase three. First-time managers at a fast-growing startup are one of the highest-risk points in the people system. I'll know who needs what before I build the program.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Audit onboarding and begin redesign">
        Onboarding at a 65-person company that grew fast is almost always inconsistent. Some employees had a great first month; others were dropped in and figured it out. I'll interview a cohort of employees who joined in the last 12 months, map the variance, and begin redesigning onboarding around what actually sets people up for success at Mintlify specifically — not a generic 30-60-90 template.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Tip>
      If I had to focus on one thing in days 31–60, it would be standing up the weekly people-health read. Everything else in this phase is important, but the exec team's ability to act on people data in real time is what separates companies that spot retention risk from companies that find out about it in exit interviews. Get this artifact right early, and the rest of the listening infrastructure builds on top of it.
    </Tip>
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Days 61–90">
    Phase three is where the foundation starts paying off. The listening infrastructure is live, the audits are complete, and I have enough context to make high-confidence recommendations and execute on the quick wins that have the most visible impact. This phase is also about demonstrating the pattern: listen, analyze, build, automate, improve.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Launch initial manager coaching program">
        A structured framework, not ad hoc conversations. Based on the manager map from phase two, I'll run the first cohort of the manager development program — likely monthly sessions, a mix of group and individual coaching, with clear goals and a feedback loop built in. I'll measure it from day one so we know whether it's working. First-time managers at Mintlify are going to scale into significant people leadership as the company grows; investing in them now is one of the highest-return things the People function can do.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Deliver compensation review recommendations to founders and Finance">
        The full output of the compensation audit: where we have gaps, where we have risk, what it costs to fix the most critical issues, and what the comp philosophy should be as the company grows into its next stage. I'll bring a point of view on equity refresh strategy, job architecture, and how to build comp bands that hold up at 150 employees, not just 65. This is a conversation with the founders and Finance, not a presentation — I'll come with a recommendation and expect to pressure-test it.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Complete first iteration of performance cycle audit">
        A clear picture of how the current performance cycle works, where it breaks down, and what it would look like redesigned. I'll present the findings and proposed changes to the founders — including the hard ones, like whether the current cycle is actually producing useful signal or just producing paperwork. I won't redesign the full cycle in 90 days, but I'll have a roadmap for the redesign that the founders can react to and resource.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Automate at least one high-touch manual process">
        Working with the ops team, I'll identify the manual process in the people stack that is consuming the most time relative to its complexity. Based on my experience automating comp letter distribution, equity processing, and payroll workflows at OpenAI and Tools for Humanity, I have a strong prior on where these usually live. I'll scope, build, and ship at least one automation by day 90 — a concrete demonstration that the People function is going to reduce administrative drag, not add to it.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Publish updated employee handbook with critical compliance additions">
        The remediation work from phase two lands here: an updated handbook that reflects the company Mintlify actually is, addresses the compliance gaps identified in the audit, and is written in language employees will actually read. Not a legal document disguised as a culture document.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Deliver 90-day retrospective to founders with roadmap for months 4–12">
        An honest accounting of the first 90 days: what I found, what I built, what I got wrong, and what the plan is from here. Months 4–12 is where the bigger structural work happens — full performance cycle redesign, compensation band rollout, manager program expansion, and the hiring infrastructure to support the next phase of growth. By day 90, I'll have enough context to make that roadmap specific and credible.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    <Tip>
      If I had to focus on one thing in days 61–90, it would be the compensation review. It has the most direct impact on retention risk in the near term, it signals to employees that the People function is paying attention to the things that matter, and it gives the founders a concrete, data-grounded input for financial planning. Everything else in this phase matters, but getting comp right unlocks the other work.
    </Tip>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
